BakeOut

Scope · approved

A Flutter dating app where people match over how they cook and eat, and a cherry-shaped AI wingman takes a new match from "hi" to a planned virtual cooking date — then leaves them to it.

The Unique Kernel

After a match, the AI wingman steps in as a middle person, breaks the ice, and keeps pushing until the two people have a virtual cooking date set: a fixed time, a dish they both confirmed, the recipe, and a song. Then it leaves the chat forever.

"Now it's on you two how you bake this out further."

Green steps set the stage. Red steps are the kernel, and they're the "oh, that's cool" beat the demo video has to land. Two real people on two phones, in one live chat with the wingman.

Who It's For

An introvert who isn't a small-talk person and loves food. On today's apps they find it hard to get a match, and after matching it's "so hard to think of even one reply." They swipe, stall on the first message, and the match goes nowhere.

Early signal
23 of 25

people the team shared the idea with in the past 2–3 days said they want to try it. The team found nothing close to it among existing dating apps.

Inspiration & Identity

  • Mascot: a cherry wingman — male cherry on his side, female cherry on hers.
  • Feel: comfortable and calming for someone dating apps overwhelm. Fun, warm, food-led.
  • Tone: playful food language — "bake this out."
  • Insight: eating habits reflect a person more deeply than random talk.

Why This Matters

Vinamra is an introvert who finds current dating apps too hard: "despite all the development the core of dating apps never really upgraded." Both he and Subu love cooking, and they want to "build something I wish should have existed."

What "Working" Looks Like

Two real people on two phones. Each sets up a food-centered profile and passes picture verification, they see each other, swipe right, and match instantly. The cherry wingman appears in a shared chat on both sides, starts the conversation, helps them get to know each other, then sets up the date: picks a dish from their choices, confirms it with both, posts the recipe, suggests a song, and fixes a time. It signs off and leaves. The two keep chatting without it.

The demo video wraps this in an opening skit (two people tired of dating apps, bored while cooking, nudged by a WhatsApp-style message) and a behind-the-scenes look at the end. That's video content for 6-ship, not app features.

Open Questions for Later Phases

  • Picture verification: what exactly counts as "verified" (3-prd), and whether it runs on the phone or through an outside service, plus how face data is handled (4-spec).
  • Wingman behavior: what it asks to help them get to know each other, and how it decides it's time to move to the date (3-prd).

The Boundary

Now: the proof of concept
  • Quick food-centered profile setup (cooking and eating habits).
  • Picture verification during setup, so there are no fake people or profiles. A real build, not simulated. Added after the first draft; the wingman stays the priority.
  • The two users see each other, swipe right, and match. A couple of profiles is enough; no matching at scale.
  • After the match, the wingman joins a live chat between the two real users and breaks the ice.
  • The wingman guides them to a date: time, a dish both confirm, the recipe in chat, a song suggestion.
  • The wingman signs off and permanently leaves; the two users keep chatting.
  • A polished, comfortable UI with the cherry mascot. The interface is part of what the demo sells.
Later: worth doing, not now
  • Food filters for search (vegan, vegetarian, baking, and so on). May come back once the core path works.
  • Real matching across many users.
  • The virtual cooking date itself, e.g. an in-app video call.
  • Playing music in the app, rather than suggesting a song.
Explicitly cut
  • None so far.
